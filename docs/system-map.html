System map · synqology

What Provenance actually does

Every night it reads your algorithm, goes looking for research that would prove one of its numbers wrong, and — only when several strong studies agree — opens a pull request. You decide. Nothing merges itself.

The whole loop

Read top to bottom. The numbers on the arrows are from a real run last night.

Each part, in plain words

Nine components. The two shaded in the diagram are the ones doing the refusing.

01

Agenda

Reads your VitalityIndexCalculator.swift and works out what to search for. It's never given a topic list — your code is the topic list. Change a constant and the searches change with it.

02

Scout

Queries PubMed and Europe PMC for all 11 components, every night. It asks about an 18-month window rather than “yesterday”, because PubMed indexes papers weeks late. It knows what's new by remembering every paper's ID, not by date.

03

Triage

A cheap, fast model answers one question per paper: could this possibly bear on any of your scoring components? Roughly 4 in 5 get dropped here, each with a written reason.

04

Appraiser

The careful model grades each survivor: study design, sample size, follow-up, evidence tier A to D. Preprints are capped at C. Animal studies are D. For every claim it makes, it must quote the sentence it came from.

05

Grounding — the first refusal

Plain code, no model. It checks the quoted sentence really appears in the paper, and that any number attached to it appears inside that sentence.

That second check is the important one: a real quote with an invented statistic next to it passes a quote check and fails here.

06

Synthesist — the second refusal

Arithmetic, run before any model is consulted. A proposal only opens when at least 3 separate papers disagree with the same constant, at least one is high-tier, and they come from more than one research group.

And once you reject something, it stays quiet until 5 more challenging papers turn up. Your “no” sticks.

07

Engineer

Searches your live repo for where the value actually lives — the proposal often names a symbol that doesn't exist. Writes the real edit, updates the spec and the pinned tests, bumps the version, and runs your scoring suite against the change.

It works in a throwaway copy of your repo, so your 33 uncommitted files are never touched.

08

Storyteller

Turns the same evidence into slides and an animated reel. Its output form has nowhere to write a number — it picks the angle and the words, and code fills in every figure from a verified claim.

09

Health check

Runs nightly over proposals already open. Has your branch moved under the diff? Does GitHub match the decision on record? Is the companion change still missing? Has this been sitting undecided for two weeks?

Why you can trust the numbers

Six checks stand between a language model and something published under your name. Each is code with a test behind it, not an instruction in a prompt.

CheckWhat it refuses
GroundingA quote that isn't in the paper; a number that isn't in the quote
ConvergenceActing on one study, or on three from the same lab
ClaimsAny figure in the copy that no appraised study reports
Language“Reduces” on a cohort study; “dramatically”, “breakthrough”
StructureA chart with one data point; a deck that's mostly text cards
Readability“Cardiovascular”, “mortality” — jargon where a reader decides to stop

The one that surprised me

The language gate's first rejection was “Concentrated training sharply reduces cardiovascular risk.” Every figure in it was grounded and verified. It was still wrong twice: “sharply” is an intensifier the study doesn't license, and “reduces” claims cause from a study that only shows association. A wrong number is easy to catch. Overstating how strong a finding is looks completely fine — and it's how health content actually goes wrong.

The review I'm asking for

Two lines, in a second repository.

Your scoring rules live in two places: the Swift code in synq, and the explainer synqIQ reads when a user asks why their Cardio score is what it is. A pull request can't span two repos, so approving the first one alone would ship an app whose own assistant describes the old algorithm.

-  TO IMPROVE: Aim for 150+ minutes/week spread across 3+ days
+  full credit needs 2+ such days/week (day factor denominator = 2)
