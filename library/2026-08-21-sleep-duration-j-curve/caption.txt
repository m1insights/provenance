Everyone knows the 8-hour rule. The curve's low point is 7 — and the risky side isn't the one you think.

The numbers in this video: across 40 cohort studies of 2,200,425 adults, risk of dying from any cause was lowest at 7 hours of sleep a day. 8 hours itself ran +3%. Short sleepers ran +4–6%. The long side climbed: +13% at 9 hours, +25% at 10, +38% at 11 (all vs 7 hours).

Long sleep is often a marker of something else going on — an association, not proof of cause.

Study: Liu et al., Sleep Med Rev, 2016 — non-linear meta-regression of 40 prospective cohorts, 271,507 deaths; sleep counted per 24 hours, not just nighttime.
