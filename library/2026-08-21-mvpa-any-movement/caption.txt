Your workout counts. It's just not the only thing that does — and the trackers can prove it.

The numbers in this video: across 8 studies of 36,383 adults who wore motion trackers, risk of dying from any cause fell −52% one quarter up from the least active — and kept falling, −66%, then −73% in the most active quarter. That's TOTAL movement: the workout, plus the walking, the stairs, the chores, the standing up. Any intensity. All of it counted.

Zoom out and the same paper's structured-exercise ladder (moderate-to-vigorous activity alone) ran −36%, −45%, −48% — real, but smaller. The full picture only shows up when everything you do all day is on the chart.

Association in cohort data, not proof of cause — the least active group includes people who are less active because they're already unwell.

Study: Ekelund et al., BMJ, 2019 — systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis of 8 accelerometry cohorts, median follow-up 5.8 years, 2,149 deaths.
