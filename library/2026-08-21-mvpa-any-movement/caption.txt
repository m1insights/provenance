You've been told the workout is what saves you. The trackers say something better.

The numbers in this video: across 8 studies of 36,383 adults who wore motion trackers, risk of dying from any cause fell −52% one quarter up from the least active — and kept falling, −66%, then −73% in the most active quarter. That's total movement: walking, chores, standing up — any intensity, all of it counted by the tracker.

Zoom out and structured exercise alone tells a smaller story: the same paper's moderate-to-vigorous activity ladder ran −36%, −45%, −48%. Movement in any form is where most of the drop lives.

Association in cohort data, not proof of cause — the least active group includes people who are less active because they're already unwell.

Study: Ekelund et al., BMJ, 2019 — systematic review and harmonised meta-analysis of 8 accelerometry cohorts, median follow-up 5.8 years, 2,149 deaths.
