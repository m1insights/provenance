Your brain ages at its own speed — and a blood test can now read its age. What that number says about Alzheimer's surprised even the genetics.

The numbers in this video: in 44,498 UK Biobank adults, a brain older than its owner carried 3.1× the risk of Alzheimer's — similar to carrying APOE4, the strongest genetic risk factor. A brain younger than its owner carried 0.26× the risk (74% lower) — protection similar to two copies of APOE2, the luckiest genotype there is. And it held whatever genes people actually carried.

Zoom out and a young brain mattered beyond Alzheimer's: in the same study, people with youthful brains had −40% mortality risk, youthful immune systems −42%, and both together −56%.

Organ ages shifted with lifestyle factors and medications — associations in cohort data, not proof of cause.

Study: Oh et al., Nature Medicine, 2025 — plasma proteomic organ clocks (2,916 proteins, 11 organs), n=44,498, 2–17 years of follow-up.
