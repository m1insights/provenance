One blood draw. 2,916 proteins. Eleven separate ages — one for your brain, heart, liver, immune system… and the count that ages early decides more than your birthday does.

The numbers in this video: in 44,498 UK Biobank adults followed up to 17 years, people with 2–4 organs aging early had 2.3× the risk of dying of those aging normally. With 5–7 it was 4.5×. With 8 or more: 8.3×.

The flip side, from the same study: a youthful brain cut mortality risk −40%, a youthful immune system −42%, and both together −56%. An especially aged brain carried Alzheimer's risk similar to an APOE4 gene copy — and a youthful one was protective, independent of genes.

Organ ages shifted with lifestyle factors and medications — these are associations in cohort data, not proof of cause.

Study: Oh et al., Nature Medicine, 2025 — plasma proteomic organ clocks in the UK Biobank, n=44,498, 2–17 years of follow-up.
