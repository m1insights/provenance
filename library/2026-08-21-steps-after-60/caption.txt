The step counter matters more after 60, not less — and the effect is bigger than most people guess.

The numbers in this video: across 8 studies of 20,152 adults with step-counting devices, adults 60 and over in the higher step groups had −20%, −38%, then −49% lower risk of heart disease than those walking least. Half the risk, from walking.

Zoom out and the same paper found something surprising in under-60s: over the same ~6 years, no statistically clear heart-disease signal at any step level. The curve in this video is the 60+ group, where the association was clear.

Association in cohort data, not proof of cause — people who walk less may already be less well.

Study: Paluch et al., Circulation, 2022 — harmonized meta-analysis of 8 prospective device-measured cohorts, mean follow-up 6.2 years, 1,523 cardiovascular events.
